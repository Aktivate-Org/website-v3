If you're wondering how to get brand deals in the Philippines with a few thousand followers, the short answer is: brands are already looking for creators your size, but most of them won't find you by accident. You get there by making it easy for them to see who your audience is, what you can make, and what it costs.

This guide walks through the steps, from setting up your profile to sending a pitch, pricing in pesos, disclosing sponsored posts and staying right with the BIR.

## Why brands in the Philippines want smaller creators

The audience is there. DataReportal's [Digital 2026: The Philippines](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-philippines) report counts 95.8 million social media user identities in October 2025, 81.9% of the population. TikTok's ads alone reached 64.0 million adults, and Facebook 95.8 million. (These are ad-reach numbers, not unique people, but the point stands: the audience is huge and it's spread across many niches.)

For a brand, that means a skincare shop in Quezon City or a café in Cebu doesn't need a celebrity. It needs a creator whose followers live nearby, trust their recommendations and actually comment. That's where nano (roughly 1,000–10,000 followers) and micro creators (roughly 10,000–100,000) come in.

## Step 1: Pick a niche you can post about every week

Brands pay for an audience, not just a follower count. "Lifestyle" is hard to sell. "Budget meal prep for Manila office workers" or "commuter-friendly fashion in Davao" is easy, because a brand can picture exactly who will see it.

A good niche is:

- **Specific:** a topic plus a type of person (and, if you can, a place).
- **Repeatable:** you can make content about it every week without running out.
- **Buyable:** there are brands selling things to that audience.

## Step 2: Make your profile brand-ready

Before you pitch anyone, look at your profile the way a marketing manager would.

- **Bio:** one line on what you post, your city and a business email. "📍Cebu · affordable eats & weekend trips · collabs: name@email.com" works.
- **Pinned posts:** your three best pieces of content, ideally ones that look like what a brand would want.
- **Consistency:** post on a regular schedule for at least a few weeks before pitching. An inactive account is the fastest way to get skipped.
- **Engagement:** brands check comments and saves, not just likes. Work out your rate with the [engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator/) so you can quote it.

## Step 3: Build a one-page media kit

A media kit is a short PDF or link that answers a brand's questions before they ask. Keep it to one or two pages:

1. Who you are and what you post (two sentences).
2. Platforms, followers and average views per post.
3. Engagement rate.
4. Audience: top cities, age range and gender split (screenshots from your insights work).
5. Past collaborations, including unpaid or barter ones, with results if you have them.
6. What you offer: for example one TikTok video, an Instagram Reel plus three Stories, or a Facebook post.
7. Your starting rates, or "rates on request".

## Step 4: Know your rate in pesos

Many first-time creators either undercharge or freeze when a brand asks "how much?". Have a number ready. Start with the [influencer rate calculator for the Philippines](/tools/influencer-rate-calculator/?country=PH), which gives a range in pesos by platform and follower count, then adjust for:

- **Usage rights:** if the brand wants to run your video as an ad, charge more.
- **Exclusivity:** if you can't work with competitors for a period, charge more.
- **Effort:** a scripted, edited video with a product shoot takes more time than a Story.

Barter deals (free product for a post) are common at the start. They're fine when you'd genuinely use the product and the brand is one you'd like on your page, but agree in writing what you're delivering. Free products still count as income for tax purposes (more on that below).

## Step 5: Find brands and pitch them

There are three main routes.

**Pitch brands directly.** Make a list of 20 local brands you already use or would happily recommend, especially small and growing ones. Send a short message by email or DM:

> Hi [Brand], I'm [name], I post [niche] for an audience mostly in [city], aged [range]. My last three videos averaged [X] views and my engagement rate is [Y]%. I'd love to create a [format] showing [specific idea] for your [product]. Media kit attached. Would you be open to a collab this month?

Specific ideas get replies. "Let's collab!" doesn't.

**Join platform marketplaces.** TikTok One is TikTok's own marketplace for brand work. According to TikTok's help page [About TikTok One for creators](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/about-tiktok-one-for-creators?lang=en), you need to be at least 18, follow the Community Guidelines and Branded Content Policy, and TikTok One has to be available in your country or region. The [sign-up page](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/how-creators-can-sign-up-for-tiktok-one?lang=en) says creators need at least 1,000 followers to take part in projects, and that requirements vary by project. You can add your starting rates and the industries you work in to your profile.

**Use creator platforms.** Platforms like Aktivate list campaigns from brands and let you apply to the ones that fit (more on that at the end).

## Step 6: Plan around the shopping calendar

Brand budgets follow the calendar. In the Philippines, the long Christmas season, the 11.11 and 12.12 sales, back-to-school, Valentine's Day and summer travel are all moments when brands look for content. Pitch a few weeks ahead with a seasonal idea ("a ₱1,000 Noche Buena spread using your products") instead of waiting until the week itself, when the good slots are already taken.

## Step 7: Disclose every sponsored post

There's no single influencer law in the Philippines, but sponsored content passed off as an honest personal opinion can run into the Consumer Act. A March 2026 [commentary by Respicio & Co.](https://www.respicio.ph/commentaries/philippine-laws-on-social-media-influencer-marketing-endorsements-and-consumer-protection) explains that any "material connection" with a brand (payment, free products, commission, trips) should be disclosed clearly and close to the endorsement. Labels like "Paid partnership with [Brand]", "Sponsored", "Ad" or "I received this for free from [Brand]" are clear. Vague tags like "Thanks [Brand]!", #sp or #collab, or a disclosure buried at the end of a long caption or in the comments, are risky. The same commentary notes that brands often write Ad Standards Council compliance into their contracts.

Use the platform's own paid-partnership label when it exists. It protects you, and audiences trust creators who are upfront.

## Step 8: Get your taxes in order

The BIR's Revenue Memorandum Circular 97-2021 made it clear that income from social media work is taxable. As [PwC Philippines](https://www.pwc.com/ph/en/tax/tax-publications/taxwise-or-otherwise/2021/when-the-bir-hits-the-notification-bell.html) and [Grant Thornton](https://www.grantthornton.com.ph/insights/articles-and-updates1/tax-notes/taxation-of-social-media-influencers-income/) summarise it:

- Payments for sponsored posts, ambassador deals and affiliate commissions are business income.
- Free products and services you receive must be declared at their fair market value.
- You need a TIN and registration with your Revenue District Office, and you should keep registered books of accounts.
- Self-employed individuals with gross receipts up to ₱3 million can generally opt for an 8% tax on gross receipts instead of graduated income tax plus percentage tax.

Rules and rates change, so check with your RDO or an accountant before your first big deal.

## FAQ

### How many followers do I need to get brand deals in the Philippines?

There's no fixed number. Many small brands work with nano creators who have a few thousand engaged followers. On TikTok One, TikTok says creators need at least 1,000 followers to take part in projects, with other requirements depending on the project.

### How much should I charge for a sponsored post?

It depends on your platform, views, engagement, niche and whether the brand wants usage rights. Use the [rate calculator for the Philippines](/tools/influencer-rate-calculator/?country=PH) for a starting range in pesos, then adjust.

### Do I have to pay tax on free products from brands?

Under BIR RMC 97-2021, as summarised by PwC and Grant Thornton, products received in exchange for content are income and should be declared at fair market value. Ask your RDO or an accountant how this applies to you.

### Do I need to label sponsored posts?

Yes, you should. Clear labels such as "Paid partnership", "Sponsored" or "Ad", placed near the endorsement, are the safe choice. Vague tags or disclosures hidden in the comments are not.
