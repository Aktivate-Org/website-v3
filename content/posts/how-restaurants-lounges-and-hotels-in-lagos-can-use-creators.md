Ask anyone in Lagos how they picked their last brunch spot, lounge or staycation, and the answer is usually the same: "I saw it on Instagram" or "it came up on my TikTok."

Food, nightlife and hotel content is some of the most watched content in the city. People save the reel, send it to the group chat, and turn up on Saturday. That makes creators one of the most direct ways for a restaurant, lounge or hotel to fill seats, tables and rooms.

The problem is that most venues do it the hard way: they invite a few big names, host them for free, get one post, and never find out whether anyone actually came because of it.

Here's how to do it properly.

## 1. Pick creators whose followers can actually visit

For a venue, location matters more than follower count. A creator with 200,000 followers spread across the country is worth less to a Lekki restaurant than one with 8,000 followers who mostly live on the Island.

Look for:

- **Food creators** for restaurants, cafés and brunch spots. They know how to make a dish look worth the trip.
- **Nightlife and lifestyle creators** for lounges, rooftops and event nights.
- **Travel, staycation and couples creators** for hotels, short-lets and day passes.
- **Neighbourhood creators**: people who post about Lekki, Victoria Island, Ikeja, Surulere or Yaba specifically. Their followers are your walk-ins.

Before you agree anything, ask for a screenshot of their audience insights showing top cities. You want Lagos near the top. Then check their engagement with our free [engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator/): a smaller account with real comments and saves will beat a big account with quiet posts.

## 2. Ask for content that makes people want to come

The best hospitality content answers one question for the viewer: "What would my night (or meal, or weekend) here actually feel like?" Brief creators on the experience, not on a list of things to say.

**Restaurants and cafés**
- "What I'd order here": a walk through the menu with prices on screen
- The signature dish, filmed from the first bite
- Behind the scenes with the chef, or a new menu reveal

**Lounges and rooftops**
- "A night at…" reels that capture the music, the crowd and the view
- Event night announcements and recaps (theme nights, live DJ sets, game nights)
- Table or bottle packages explained clearly, with the price

**Hotels, short-lets and resorts**
- Room tours and "24 hours at…" staycation vlogs
- Pool day passes, brunch and spa packages
- Weekend deals for couples, friends or families

Always ask creators to include the practical details people need to act: the location, opening hours, price range and how to book. A beautiful reel without "how do I get a table?" leaves money on the table.

## 3. Decide how you'll pay them

There are three common ways, and you can mix them.

**Hosted experience.** You cover the meal, the night or the stay in exchange for content. This works well with smaller creators, but be clear about what you expect in return (for example, one reel and three stories within seven days) and put it in writing.

**Flat fee.** You pay a set amount per post or package. This is normal for bigger creators and for content you need on a specific date, like an event launch. **Commission per booking.** You pay creators only when their followers book or buy. This is the lowest-risk option for you, and it rewards the creators who actually bring guests through the door.

## 4. Track what each creator brings in

If you can't see which creator sent which guest, you'll keep paying for content that doesn't fill a single table. A few simple ways to track it:

- Give each creator their own **booking link or code** ("Show this at the door for a free mocktail", "Use TOLU10 when you book").
- Ask your front desk or reservations team to note **"how did you hear about us?"** on every booking.
- Count redemptions per creator every week, and keep working with the ones who deliver.

## 5. Follow the rules

Influencer posts are adverts in Nigeria, and the Advertising Regulatory Council of Nigeria (ARCON) expects them to be treated that way. Sponsored posts should be clearly disclosed, for example with "#ad" or the platform's paid partnership label, and ARCON has said adverts, including influencer content, should be approved before they go out. Agree with your creators who will handle this before the campaign starts.

If your venue serves alcohol, keep drinks content responsible: no one who looks under 18, no encouraging excessive drinking, and make it clear that drinks are for adults only. Instagram and TikTok also have their own rules for alcohol content, so brief creators on them.

## 6. Start before December

December is the busiest month of the year for Lagos hospitality. Detty December brings in the diaspora, the concerts and the end-of-year parties, and the best creators get booked up early. If you want creators talking about your venue in December, the time to brief them is now.

A simple plan:

1. **This week:** shortlist 10 to 20 creators who post about your area and type of venue.
2. **Next week:** host or brief your first five, each with their own booking link or code.
3. **Through December:** keep the ones whose followers actually book, and double down on them.
