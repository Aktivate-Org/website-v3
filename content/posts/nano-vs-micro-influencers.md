When most brands think about influencer marketing, they think of big names: the celebrity with millions of followers and a fee to match.

But many of the campaigns that actually move products, especially in Nigeria and across Africa, are run with much smaller creators. Nano and micro influencers often cost a fraction of a big name, feel more trustworthy to their followers, and can be booked in numbers.

So which should you work with? Here's how they differ and how to choose.

## What counts as nano and micro?

There's no official definition, but most of the industry uses roughly these ranges:

| Tier | Followers | What they're like |
|---|---|---|
| **Nano** | About 1,000 to 10,000 | Everyday people with a close, often local following. Friends, classmates, colleagues and neighbours. |
| **Micro** | About 10,000 to 100,000 | Creators with a clear niche (beauty, food, tech, parenting) and an audience that follows them for it. |
| **Mid-tier** | About 100,000 to 500,000 | Established creators who often have management and fixed rates. |
| **Macro and celebrity** | 500,000+ | Big names, broad reach, premium fees. |

Treat these as guides, not rules. A creator with 8,000 highly engaged followers in your exact niche can be worth more than one with 80,000 who post about everything.

## Nano influencers: close, trusted, local

**Where they shine**

- **Trust:** their followers often know them personally, so a recommendation feels like advice from a friend.
- **Engagement:** smaller accounts often get a higher share of likes, comments and saves.
- **Cost:** many will work for a modest fee, free product, or commission on sales.
- **Local reach:** great for businesses that serve one city or area, like restaurants, salons, gyms and shops.

**What to watch**

- Each one reaches a small number of people, so you need several for meaningful reach.
- Content quality varies. Give a clear, simple brief.
- They may be new to brand work, so be clear about what you expect and when you'll pay.

## Micro influencers: niche authority with real reach

**Where they shine**

- **Niche credibility:** their audience follows them for a specific topic, so a product in that niche feels natural.
- **Content quality:** many make polished content that you can also use in your own ads (agree usage rights first).
- **Balance:** more reach than nano creators while still feeling personal.

**What to watch**

- Fees are higher, and the best ones are booked up in busy seasons like December.
- Check that engagement is real. A big follower count with very few comments is a warning sign.

## Which should you choose?

It depends on what you want the campaign to do.

| If you want… | Start with |
|---|---|
| Sales and sign-ups on a small budget | Many nano creators, ideally paid on commission |
| Footfall to a local business | Nano creators in your area |
| Credibility in a specific niche | Micro creators in that niche |
| Content you can reuse in ads | Micro creators with strong production quality |
| Fast awareness for a launch | A mix: a few micro creators plus many nano creators |

For most growing African brands, the strongest approach is a **mix**: a handful of micro creators to anchor the campaign, plus a larger group of nano creators to spread it widely and drive sales.

## The bottom line

Bigger isn't automatically better. Nano influencers bring trust and local reach at low cost. Micro influencers bring niche authority and better content. Most brands get the best results by combining them, and by measuring sales, not just likes.
