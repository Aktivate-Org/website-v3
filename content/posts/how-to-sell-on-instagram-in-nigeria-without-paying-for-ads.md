For many Nigerian businesses, Instagram is the shop. Customers find you there, ask "how much?" in your DMs, and pay by transfer.

But with ad costs rising and the naira under pressure, paying to boost every post isn't realistic for most small businesses. The good news is that you can still grow sales on Instagram without an ad budget. It takes a clear profile, consistent content, fast replies, and other people talking about you.

Here's the playbook.

## 1. Turn your profile into a storefront

Most people who land on your profile decide in a few seconds whether to follow, message or leave. Make it easy for them.

- **Switch to a professional (business) account** so you get insights, contact buttons and other business tools.
- **Say what you sell and where you deliver** in the first line of your bio: "Handmade leather bags · Delivery across Nigeria".
- **Add one clear link** to where people can buy, whether that's your website, WhatsApp catalogue, Bumpa or Selar store, or a link-in-bio page.
- **Use highlights as your menu:** Prices, New arrivals, Reviews, Delivery, How to order.
- **Pin your three best posts**, such as your bestseller, your best customer review and how to order.

## 2. Post content that sells without feeling like an ad

People don't follow businesses to see flyers. They follow because the content is useful, interesting or fun. The sale comes after.

Content that works for Nigerian businesses:

- **Show the product being used:** try-ons, before and afters, unboxings, the product in a real home or kitchen
- **Behind the scenes:** packing orders, making the product, sourcing materials, your team at work
- **Answer the questions you get in DMs:** sizes, delivery times, how to use it, how it compares
- **Customer reviews and reposts:** screenshots of happy messages (with permission) and customers' own videos
- **Prices on screen:** "how much?" is the most common comment. Answer it before they ask

Reels are still the best way to reach people who don't follow you yet. Post short videos regularly, use clear text on screen, and put the hook in the first two seconds. If your account has trial reels, you can test a video with non-followers before sharing it with your followers.

## 3. Use stories to close the sale

Reels bring new people in. Stories are where your existing followers decide to buy.

- Post every day, even if it's just two or three frames
- Use polls and question stickers ("Which colour should we restock?")
- Add link stickers to products and to WhatsApp
- Announce restocks, limited quantities and delivery deadlines
- Share every customer who tags you

## 4. Reply fast, and make ordering easy

Many Instagram sales in Nigeria are won or lost in the DMs. A customer who waits three hours for a price often buys from someone else.

- Set up saved replies for prices, delivery fees and how to pay
- Add your price list and delivery information to highlights
- Move serious buyers to WhatsApp or a checkout link quickly
- Follow up politely with people who asked but didn't pay

## 5. Grow with collabs and other people's audiences

The fastest free growth on Instagram comes from being seen on other accounts.

- **Collab posts:** co-post a Reel with a complementary business (a bakery and a florist, a fashion brand and a stylist) so it appears on both profiles
- **Giveaways with partners**, where entry means following both accounts
- **Customer content:** ask happy customers to post and tag you, and repost them
- **Lives** with a creator, stylist, chef or expert in your space

## 7. Track what's working

Without ads, your time is the budget, so spend it on what works.

- Check Instagram insights weekly: which posts brought profile visits, link taps and messages
- Ask every customer "how did you find us?"
- Use a different discount code or link for each creator or partner
- Do more of what brings orders, and stop what doesn't

## A simple weekly plan

- **Three Reels** a week, at least one showing the product in use
- **Stories every day**, with at least one link or poll
- **One collab, customer repost or creator post** a week
- **Replies within the hour** during working hours

Ads can speed things up later. But plenty of Nigerian businesses are built on Instagram with good content, quick replies and other people talking about them. Start with that.
