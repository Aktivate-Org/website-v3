If you've scrolled TikTok or Instagram lately, you've seen a lot of UGC, even if you didn't know it had a name. Someone talking straight to camera about a skincare product. A quick unboxing. A "things I wish I knew before buying this" video.

Brands love it because it looks and feels real. Creators love it because you can get paid without a big following.

Here's what UGC is, and how both sides can use it.

## What does UGC mean?

**UGC stands for user-generated content**: photos, videos, reviews and posts about a product made by people rather than by the brand itself.

The term covers two related things:

1. **Organic UGC:** content real customers post on their own, like a review, an unboxing video or a photo tagging your brand. Nobody paid them.
2. **Paid UGC:** content a brand pays a creator to make in that same natural, customer-style look. The brand then posts it on its own pages or uses it in ads.

When people talk about "UGC creators" today, they usually mean the second kind.

## UGC vs influencer marketing

They overlap, but they aren't the same.

| | UGC | Influencer marketing |
|---|---|---|
| **What the brand pays for** | The content itself | Access to the creator's audience |
| **Where it's posted** | Usually the brand's own pages and ads | The creator's own account |
| **Does follower count matter?** | Not much | Yes, a lot |
| **What makes it good** | It looks real and makes people want to buy | The creator's audience trusts them |

Many creators do both: a UGC video for a brand's ads, plus a post on their own page for an extra fee.

## Why brands use UGC

- **It feels trustworthy.** People are more likely to believe someone like them than a polished brand ad.
- **It performs well in ads.** Natural, phone-filmed videos often blend into feeds better than studio content.
- **It's cheaper than a shoot.** You can get several videos from different creators for less than one professional production.
- **It gives you variety.** Test different hooks, faces and angles to see what sells.

## Common types of UGC

- **Testimonials:** "Here's why I switched to…"
- **Unboxings:** opening the product and first reactions
- **How-tos and tutorials:** showing exactly how to use the product
- **Before and after:** visible results (keep these honest)
- **Problem and solution:** "If you struggle with X, try this"
- **Day in the life:** the product appearing naturally in someone's routine
- **Reviews and comparisons:** honest pros and cons

## For brands: how to get UGC

1. **Start with your customers.** Ask happy buyers to post and tag you, and repost them (always ask permission first).
2. **Hire UGC creators for specific videos.** Give a clear brief: the product, the key message, the hook ideas, the length and the format (usually vertical 9:16).
3. **Agree usage rights in writing.** Say where you'll use the content (organic posts, paid ads), for how long, and whether you can edit it.
4. **Order a few variations.** Different hooks and openings let you find the version that sells best.
5. **Track performance.** Compare UGC against your usual creative in ads and on your pages.

## For creators: how to start doing UGC

You don't need a big following to make UGC. You need to make videos that look natural and make people want the product.

1. **Make a few sample videos** with products you already own, in different styles: an unboxing, a review, a tutorial.
2. **Build a simple portfolio**, such as a pinned highlight, a Google Drive folder or a page with your best samples.
3. **Learn the basics:** good natural light, clear audio, a strong first two seconds, text on screen, and vertical framing.
4. **Pitch brands directly**, and apply to UGC campaigns on the [Collaboration Hub](https://app.useaktivate.com).
5. **Price per video, plus usage.** Charge separately for raw footage, extra hooks, and the right to use your video in paid ads, and for how long.

## A note on honesty and disclosure

UGC works because it feels real, so keep it real. Don't fake results, don't claim you've used a product for months if you haven't, and follow the platform's rules. If the content is posted on your own account as a paid partnership, disclose it clearly. In Nigeria, advertising rules apply to influencer and creator content too.
