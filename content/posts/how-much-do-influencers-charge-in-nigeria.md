If you've ever asked a few Nigerian creators for their rates, you'll know the answers can be all over the place. One creator with 20,000 followers asks for ₦50,000. Another with the same following asks for ₦400,000.

There's no official rate card, but there are clear patterns. This guide shows you what creators typically charge, what drives the price, and how to plan a budget so your money goes into creators who actually move your product.

## Typical rates in Nigeria (2026)

These are typical ranges for **one Instagram feed post** at average engagement in a general lifestyle niche. You can get an estimate for any size, platform and niche with our [influencer rate calculator for brands](/tools/influencer-rate-calculator/?country=NG&mode=brand).

| Creator size | Followers | Typical rate for one feed post |
|---|---|---|
| Nano | around 1,000 | ₦8,000 to ₦15,000 |
| Nano to micro | around 10,000 | ₦40,000 to ₦80,000 |
| Micro | around 50,000 | ₦150,000 to ₦300,000 |
| Mid-tier | 100,000 to 500,000 | roughly ₦200,000 to ₦1,000,000 |
| Macro and celebrity | 500,000+ | from about ₦1,000,000, often much more |

The mid-tier range comes from a 2026 industry report on Nigerian creator rates. Above that level, prices depend heavily on the person, and celebrities and top skit makers set their own rates.

## What changes the price

**Format.** A Reel or TikTok takes more work than a static post and usually costs about 1.3 to 1.5 times as much. A set of stories usually costs about half the price of a feed post. A dedicated YouTube video can cost three to four times as much.

**Niche.** Creators in tech, finance and business usually charge the most, because their audiences are valuable to advertisers. Fashion, beauty and fitness creators are typically a little above lifestyle and comedy creators.

**Engagement.** A creator with highly engaged followers is worth more than one with a big, quiet following. Check before you pay with our free [engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator/).

**Production.** Scripts, multiple looks, locations, extra people on camera or professional editing all add cost.

**Management.** Creators with managers or agencies usually charge more, and may add a fee on top.

## Extras you'll be asked to pay for

Agree these up front so there are no surprises:

- **Usage or ad rights:** to run the creator's content as your own paid ad. Usually around 30% extra for 30 days.
- **Exclusivity:** the creator won't work with your competitors for a period. Usually around 20% extra for 30 days.
- **Link in bio:** around 10% extra for a week.
- **Raw footage:** around 15% extra.
- **Extra revisions** beyond what you agreed, and rush fees for short deadlines.

## Sample budgets

Here's roughly what different budgets can buy, using Reels from creators in a general niche:

| Budget | What it could look like |
|---|---|
| **₦150,000** | 2 to 3 nano creators, one Reel each |
| **₦500,000** | 4 to 6 creators at around 10,000 followers, one Reel each, or a mix of micro and nano |
| **₦1,500,000** | 2 to 3 micro creators plus 8 to 10 nano creators |
| **₦5,000,000+** | A mid-tier creator to anchor a launch, plus a group of micro and nano creators |

For most growing brands, **several smaller creators beat one big name**. You reach audiences who trust the person posting, and you spread your risk across many creators instead of betting on one post.

## How to avoid overpaying

1. **Ask for insights, not just follower counts:** reach, average views, audience location and engagement for the last month.
2. **Check audience location.** If you only deliver in Lagos, a creator whose followers are mostly abroad is expensive reach.
3. **Ask for past results:** clicks, sales or sign-ups from similar campaigns.
4. **Start small.** Test a few creators, then put more money behind the ones who perform.
5. **Give each creator a unique code or link** so you can see who actually drives sales.
6. **Put everything in writing:** deliverables, deadlines, approval, usage rights and payment terms.

Nigerian creator rates range from a few thousand naira to millions, depending on size, format, niche and engagement. Know the typical ranges, pay for the extras you really need, test with smaller creators first, and measure sales, not just views. That's how your creator budget turns into revenue.
