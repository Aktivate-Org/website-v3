"How much do you charge?" is the question most Nigerian creators dread. Quote too high and the brand disappears. Quote too low and you're stuck at that price for every deal after.

There's no official price list for influencers in Nigeria, but there are clear patterns in what brands pay and why. This guide gives you realistic starting ranges, explains what moves your rate up or down, and shows you how to put together a quote you can defend.

## Typical rates in Nigeria (2026)

These are starting ranges for **one Instagram feed post** at average engagement, in a general lifestyle niche. They're the same figures our [influencer rate calculator](/tools/influencer-rate-calculator/?country=NG) is built on.

| Followers | Typical rate for one feed post |
|---|---|
| 1,000 | ₦8,000 to ₦15,000 |
| 10,000 | ₦40,000 to ₦80,000 |
| 50,000 | ₦150,000 to ₦300,000 |

Above 50,000 followers, rates vary much more widely, depending on your niche, your track record and whether you have management. One 2026 industry report put mid-tier Nigerian creators, with 100,000 to 500,000 followers, at roughly ₦200,000 to ₦1,000,000 per sponsored post.

Use these as a starting point, not a ceiling. Your engagement, niche and results can justify charging more.

## How format changes the price

Different formats take different amounts of work and deliver different results, so price them differently. Compared with a feed post:

| Format | Typical price compared with a feed post |
|---|---|
| Instagram stories (3 frames) | About half |
| Instagram carousel | A little more |
| Instagram Reel | About 1.5 times |
| TikTok video | About 1.3 to 1.5 times |
| YouTube Short | About 1.2 times |
| 60-second mention in a YouTube video | About 2 times |
| Dedicated YouTube video | About 3 to 4 times |

So if your feed post is ₦60,000, a Reel might be around ₦85,000 to ₦90,000, and three stories around ₦30,000.

## What pushes your rate up (or down)

**Engagement.** This matters more than follower count. If your engagement rate is well above average for your size, you can charge more. If it's below, start lower and grow it. Work yours out with our free [engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator/).

**Niche.** Brands pay more to reach audiences that spend money in their category. Tech, finance and business creators usually command the highest rates, followed by fashion, beauty and fitness.

**Audience location.** If most of your followers are in Lagos or Abuja and the brand sells there, you're worth more to them. If your audience is spread across many countries and the brand only delivers in Nigeria, much of your reach is wasted for them.

**Proof of results.** Screenshots of clicks, sales or sign-ups from past campaigns are the strongest reason to charge more.

**Production.** Scripted Reels, multiple outfits, locations, or other people on camera all add time and cost. Price them in.

## Charge extra for these

Many creators give these away for free. Don't. Common add-ons and what creators typically add on top of their base rate:

- **Ad rights (usage):** the brand runs your content as a paid ad. Add around 30% for 30 days, more for longer.
- **Exclusivity:** you don't work with competing brands for a period. Add around 20% for 30 days.
- **Link in bio:** keeping the brand's link in your bio. Add around 10% for 7 days.
- **Raw footage:** sending the unedited clips. Add around 15%.
- **Extra revisions** beyond the one or two you agreed.
- **Rush jobs** with less than a few days' notice.

## How to work out your own rate

1. **Start with the range for your follower size** from the table above, or use the [rate calculator](/tools/influencer-rate-calculator/?country=NG) for your exact numbers, platform and niche.
2. **Adjust for engagement:** move up if you're above average, down if you're below.
3. **Adjust for niche and audience fit.**
4. **Multiply for the format** the brand wants.
5. **Add extras** like usage rights or exclusivity.
6. **Round to a clean number** and put it in writing.

## How to quote a brand

When a brand asks for your rates, send a short, clear quote rather than a single number:

> Thanks for reaching out! For this campaign I'd suggest:
>
> - 1 Instagram Reel: ₦90,000
> - 3 stories with a link sticker: ₦30,000
> - Package price for both: ₦110,000
>
> Ad rights for 30 days are available for an extra 30%. My media kit with recent results is attached.

Packages make it easy for the brand to say yes, and give you room to negotiate without dropping your core rate.

## When a brand can't pay your rate

Small Nigerian businesses often have tight budgets. You don't have to say no, but don't simply drop your price either. Instead:

- **Offer a smaller package:** stories instead of a Reel
- **Mix product and fee:** a lower fee plus the product
- **Take commission:** earn a percentage of every sale you drive

## Common pricing mistakes

- **Saying "whatever you can pay".** It signals you don't value your work.
- **Charging the same for every format.** A Reel is more work than a story.
- **Giving away usage rights.** If your content runs as an ad, you should be paid for it.
- **Never raising your rates.** Review them every few months as your audience and results grow.
- **Not getting it in writing.** Agree deliverables, deadlines and payment terms before you start.
