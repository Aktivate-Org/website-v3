A lot of Nigerian creators believe brand deals only start once you hit some big follower number. So they wait. They post, they grow slowly, and they watch bigger accounts get the campaigns.

The truth is that many brands, especially small and growing Nigerian businesses, actively prefer smaller creators. You cost less, your followers trust you more, and you're more likely to reply to their message.

What you need isn't a huge following. You need to look like a safe, useful bet. Here's how.

## 1. Be clear about what you're known for

Brands don't pay for "content". They pay to reach a specific kind of person. The clearer you are about who your audience is, the easier it is for a brand to say yes.

Ask yourself:

- What do I post about most? Beauty, food, tech, fashion, parenting, fitness, money, comedy?
- Who watches? Students, young professionals, new mums, people in Lagos, people in the diaspora?
- What do people ask me about in my comments and DMs?

Then say it plainly in your bio: "Affordable skincare for oily skin · Lagos" tells a brand far more than "Content creator · Living my best life".

## 2. Make your profile brand-ready

Before you pitch anyone, look at your profile the way a brand manager would. In about ten seconds, they decide whether you look professional.

- A clear profile picture and a bio that says what you do and where you are
- A business or creator email in your bio
- Your best work pinned to the top
- Recent, consistent posts (a profile that went quiet two months ago looks risky)
- At least a few posts that show you can talk about a product naturally, even if nobody paid you for them

We wrote a whole post on this: [your profile might be why brands keep skipping you](https://useaktivate.com/blog/your-profile-might-be-why-brands-keep-skipping-you/).

## 3. Know your numbers

Brands will ask for your stats, so have them ready. The ones that matter most:

- **Engagement rate:** how many people like, comment, share and save compared with your followers. Small accounts often beat big ones here. Work yours out with our free [engagement rate calculator](/tools/engagement-rate-calculator/).
- **Average views** on your Reels or TikToks over the last month
- **Audience location, age and gender** from your insights
- **Results from past work:** clicks, sales, sign-ups or messages a brand received because of you

Screenshot these and keep them up to date.

## 4. Put together a simple media kit

A media kit is a one or two-page summary you can send to any brand. Keep it simple:

1. Who you are and what you post about, in two lines
2. Your platforms, follower counts and average views
3. Your engagement rate and audience breakdown
4. Brands you've worked with (including unpaid collaborations, if they went well)
5. What you offer: Reels, TikToks, stories, UGC videos, product reviews
6. How to reach you

You can make one in Canva in an afternoon. Update it every month.

## 5. Find brands that already fit

Start with brands that already make sense for your audience:

- Products you genuinely use and have posted about
- Nigerian brands in your niche that already work with creators (look at who other creators in your niche tag)
- Small businesses on Instagram and TikTok that are growing but don't have big marketing teams yet
- Brands running campaigns on the [Aktivate Collaboration Hub](https://app.useaktivate.com), where businesses post paid campaigns and creators apply directly

Make a list of 20. Most pitches go unanswered, so you need volume.

## 6. Send a pitch that's easy to say yes to

Keep it short, specific and about them:

> Hi [name], I'm [your name], a [niche] creator in [city]. My followers are mostly [who they are], and they keep asking me about [problem the brand solves].
>
> I'd love to make a Reel showing how I use [product] in my [routine/day/kitchen]. My last [similar post] reached [number] people and got [result].
>
> Here's my media kit. Would you be open to a paid collaboration this month?

Follow up once after five to seven days. Then move on.

## 7. Set your rates with confidence

Small creators often undercharge, then feel stuck at that price. Look at what's normal for your follower size and niche with our [influencer rate calculator](/tools/influencer-rate-calculator/?country=NG), and price each deliverable separately: a Reel, a set of stories, usage rights for the brand's ads.

If a brand can't pay much yet, you can still say yes on your terms: a smaller package, a product plus a fee, or commission on sales. Avoid working for "exposure" alone.

## 9. Deliver work that gets you rebooked

The first deal is the hardest. After that, most of your income comes from brands coming back. To make that happen:

- Agree in writing what you'll deliver, by when, and how you'll be paid
- Send drafts on time, and take feedback without drama
- Disclose the partnership clearly (#ad or the platform's paid partnership label)
- Send a short report afterwards with your screenshots and results
- Thank them, and suggest what you could do next

Brands talk to each other. Being easy to work with is one of the fastest ways to grow your deals.

## Start today

Clean up your bio, work out your engagement rate, make your media kit, and send your first five pitches this week. Small creators get brand deals every day in Nigeria. The ones who get them are the ones who ask.
