Most brands can tell you how many views their last influencer campaign got. Far fewer can tell you whether it made money.

That's a problem. Without knowing your return, you can't tell which creators are worth rebooking, how much to spend next time, or whether influencer marketing is working at all.

The good news is that measuring ROI doesn't need expensive software. It needs a clear goal, a way to track results back to each creator, and a few simple formulas.

## Step 1: Decide what the campaign is for

ROI is only meaningful against a goal. Before you brief any creator, pick the one thing that matters most:

- **Sales:** orders, revenue
- **Leads or sign-ups:** app downloads, registrations, enquiries, WhatsApp messages
- **Footfall:** visits to a restaurant, shop or event
- **Awareness:** reach and new followers (usually for launches)

You can track other things too, but judge the campaign on the main goal.

## Step 2: Set up tracking before anything goes live

If you can't connect a result to a creator, you can't measure it. Set this up first:

- **A unique link per creator** (a tracked link, or a link to their own storefront)
- **A unique discount code per creator** (TOLU10, ADA10): this catches people who saw the post but bought later, or in person
- **"How did you hear about us?"** on your checkout, booking form or at the till
- **Dates:** note when each post went live, so you can spot spikes in sales and traffic

## Step 3: Collect the numbers

After the campaign (and again a few weeks later, because sales keep coming), gather:

- **What you spent:** creator fees, free products (at cost), shipping, commissions and any agency or platform fees
- **What you got:** revenue, orders, sign-ups or visits attributed to each creator
- **Supporting numbers:** reach, views, clicks and engagement for each post

## Step 4: Calculate ROI

The basic formula:

> **ROI = (Revenue from the campaign − Cost of the campaign) ÷ Cost of the campaign × 100**

**Example:** you spent ₦300,000 on five creators and tracked ₦1,050,000 in sales.

(₦1,050,000 − ₦300,000) ÷ ₦300,000 × 100 = **250% ROI**

That means every ₦1 spent brought back ₦3.50 in sales.

If your margins are thin, use **profit** instead of revenue. A campaign can bring in sales and still lose money once you take out the cost of the products.

## Step 5: Look at cost per result

When your goal isn't direct sales, these are more useful than ROI:

| Metric | Formula | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Cost per sale** | Total cost ÷ number of sales | Comparing creators on sales campaigns |
| **Cost per lead / sign-up** | Total cost ÷ leads | App, service and B2B campaigns |
| **Cost per click** | Total cost ÷ link clicks | Traffic campaigns |
| **Cost per 1,000 views (CPM)** | Total cost ÷ views × 1,000 | Comparing with what you pay for ads |
| **Engagement rate** | (Likes + comments + shares + saves) ÷ reach or followers | Judging content quality and audience interest |

## Step 6: Compare creators, not just campaigns

The most valuable thing ROI tells you is **who** is worth working with again. Put every creator in a simple table:

| Creator | Cost | Sales | Revenue | Cost per sale | ROI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| @creatorA | ₦80,000 | 32 | ₦480,000 | ₦2,500 | 500% |
| @creatorB | ₦150,000 | 9 | ₦135,000 | ₦16,667 | −10% |

It's common to find that one or two smaller creators drive most of the sales, while a bigger name with more reach sells very little. Rebook the first group, and drop or renegotiate the rest.

## Don't forget what's harder to count

Some value doesn't show up immediately in sales:

- **Content you can reuse** in your own ads (if you agreed usage rights)
- **New followers** and people who'll buy later
- **Search interest** in your brand name after a campaign
- **Social proof:** reviews and posts that make future customers trust you

Note these, but don't let them hide a campaign that clearly didn't work.

## Quick checklist

- One clear goal per campaign
- A unique link and code for every creator
- All costs written down, including free products
- Revenue (or profit) per creator, measured again after a few weeks
- ROI and cost per result for each creator
- Rebook the winners

Measure every campaign this way and, within a few months, you'll know exactly how much to spend on creators, and with whom.
